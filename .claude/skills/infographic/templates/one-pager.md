# Why does Wi-Fi slow down when everyone is online?

Because devices share one channel — and they have to *take turns*.

</div>

<div class="label">Three key ideas</div>

<div class="row">
<div class="card">
<span class="num">1</span>

### One talks at a time

On a shared channel only one device can send at any moment.

</div>
<div class="card">
<span class="num">2</span>

### Listen before you talk

A device checks whether the channel is free before sending.

</div>
<div class="card">
<span class="num">3</span>

### Collide, then wait randomly

If two send at once, both wait a *random* time and retry.

</div>
</div>

<div class="band">
<div class="myth">

More bandwidth always makes a slow network faster.

</div>
<div class="fact">

With many users, **waiting for a turn** often costs more than raw speed.

</div>
</div>

<div class="takeaway">

## Now you can

- explain why a busy network feels slow
- predict what happens when devices collide
- argue why the waiting time must be random
